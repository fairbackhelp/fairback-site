Planning to leave hospital starts during the stay, or before it for a planned admission. The NHS says family and carers can be involved in these discussions if the patient wants. This page lists what the NHS says should be in place, so you can check nothing is missed.

Before leaving, check

  • Transport home is arranged (taxi, voluntary transport or hospital transport)
  • Any medicines or new equipment are ready, and you know how to use them
  • Unpaid carers or care workers know when the person is coming home
  • Information about the stay has been shared with the GP
  • You know the next steps in care, and how to get help from the hospital
  • Local voluntary support for the first few days, if needed

Free short-term help

Some people get intermediate care to help them recover and regain independence. The NHS says it's free and usually lasts up to 6 weeks, depending on needs and progress. A needs assessment decides whether it's right.

For longer-term care needs, the local council organises a care needs assessment after the person has been home for a while.

If something doesn't seem right

  • Speak to the hospital staff who arranged the discharge
  • Get advice from the hospital's PALS (Patient Advice and Liaison Service) or the charity POhWER
  • For care at home that isn't working, contact the council and ask for a review

If you're the carer

Write down what you're told, including new medicines and who to call. You can also ask the council for a free carer's assessment.

Source: NHS, "Planning to leave hospital" (nhs.uk). This covers England; other UK nations have similar arrangements.
