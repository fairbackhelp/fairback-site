How to appeal a health insurance denial: the 5 denial types and the fix for each

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules differ by plan and state, so check your own documents and official sources, including what you read here. Law last checked: 20 November 2026.

Short answer: first work out why you were denied. The fix depends on the reason, and using the wrong fix is the most common reason people give up. Fewer than 1 in 100 denied claims are appealed, yet about a third of appeals go the patient's way (KFF, 2024).

The 5 types

  • Paperwork (missing prior authorization or referral, code 197): the doctor's office submits it, often retroactively.
  • Medical necessity (code 50): get the criteria, then a doctor's letter answering each one, then appeal.
  • Coding or billing error (codes 16, 11, 18): the provider corrects and resubmits.
  • Out-of-network or surprise bill (code 242): check whether the No Surprises Act caps what you owe.
  • Not covered or not on formulary (codes 96, 119): check the plan wording, and ask for an exception or appeal the classification.

Key deadlines (most private and employer plans)

  • Internal appeal: 180 days from the denial notice.
  • External review: 4 months from the final internal denial.
  • Urgent cases: decisions often within 72 hours (some plans allow up to 4 business days).
