What to put in a family emergency binder: a complete checklist

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Laws and services differ by state and country, so check your own documents and official sources. Last checked: November 2026.

A family emergency binder (also called a legacy binder or "in case something happens" file) gathers the information your family would need if you were suddenly ill, injured or died. It doesn't replace a will. It helps people find things and act calmly.

The sections to include

  • Emergency contacts: family, neighbors, doctors, employer, and who to call first.
  • Medical: conditions, medications and doses, allergies, insurance cards, and where any advance directive is kept.
  • Household: who lives with you, children's schools and caregivers, pets and their vet.
  • Money: banks, cards, retirement and investment accounts. Write the institution and the last 4 digits only.
  • Bills and autopay: what leaves your account each month, so nothing important lapses.
  • Insurance: life, home or renters, auto, health, disability. Insurer, policy (last 4) and claims number.
  • Documents: where the originals are: will, powers of attorney, deeds, titles, passports, birth and marriage certificates.
  • Digital life: email, phone, photos, social accounts, and where passwords are kept (a password manager), never the passwords themselves.
  • Advisers: attorney, accountant, financial adviser, insurance agent.
  • Final wishes: funeral or memorial preferences, people to tell, messages for loved ones.

Tips that make it work

  • Fill it in over a few short sessions. Start with contacts and medical, which matter most in a crisis.
  • Keep it in a fire-resistant box or locked drawer, and tell two trusted people where it is.
  • Review it once a year.
