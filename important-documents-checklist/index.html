Important documents checklist: what to keep and where to find it

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Laws and services differ by state and country, so check your own documents and official sources. Last checked: November 2026.

Most families only discover how scattered their paperwork is when they urgently need one document. A simple list of what you have, and where each original is kept, solves most of that.

The documents to list

  • Identity: passports, birth certificates, marriage or civil partnership certificates, national ID or Social Security cards.
  • Home and property: deed or lease, mortgage papers, home insurance, vehicle titles or registration.
  • Money: bank and retirement account statements (note the institution and last 4 digits only), tax returns.
  • Insurance: life, health, disability, home and auto policies, with the claims phone number.
  • Health: insurance cards, a current medicines list, and where any advance directive or medical power of attorney is kept.
  • Family and estate: will, powers of attorney, guardianship wishes for children, funeral preferences.
  • Digital: where passwords are kept (a password manager), never the passwords themselves.

Make the list useful

  • For each document, write where the original is (safe, filing cabinet, attorney's office, cloud folder).
  • Keep originals of hard-to-replace papers in a fire-resistant place.
  • Tell two trusted people where the list is, and review it once a year.
