Lost clothes, swapped slippers and missing glasses are among the most common worries for families once someone moves into a care home. Most homes wash everything together and hot, so labelling and what you pack make a real difference.

Clothes

  • Ask the home first. Some homes label clothes for you or prefer a particular label type.
  • Labels that families say last: iron-on or sew-in woven labels, and stick-on labels designed to survive washing machines and tumble dryers. Plain stick-on labels can peel off socks; sew-in labels last longest.
  • Back-up: a laundry marker on the inside label, and a photo of every item.
  • What to leave at home: wool, cashmere, silk and anything "hand wash only". Families report these coming back shrunk.
  • Pack more than you think: about 7 to 10 sets of everyday clothes, as items are often away in the laundry.

Glasses, dentures and hearing aids

  • Glasses: shrink-on or engraved name labels on the arms, and a spare pair with the optician's details.
  • Dentures: ask the dentist about name-marking the denture; use a named, brightly coloured case.
  • Hearing aids: a named case and a note in the care plan of when they go in and out. Ask the audiology service how to get replacements, as some are free through the NHS.

An inventory saves arguments later

List every item with a photo and give the home a copy. Many homes have a property book; ask to sign it.

Where to look

Iron-on name labels, sew-in woven labels, wash-proof stick-on labels, laundry marker pens, labels for glasses and denture cases. Check each product's wash temperature before you buy.

Sources: carehome.co.uk "What to pack when moving into a care home"; families' experiences on Mumsnet Elderly Parents and the Alzheimer's Society forum.
