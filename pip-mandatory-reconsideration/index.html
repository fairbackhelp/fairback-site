PIP mandatory reconsideration: how to ask, deadlines and what to write

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules change, so check your own letters and GOV.UK, including what you read here. Last checked: November 2026.

If you disagree with a PIP decision, you must first ask DWP to look again. This is a mandatory reconsideration (MR). You can only appeal to a tribunal after it.

The deadline

Ask within one month of the date on your decision letter. Late requests, up to 13 months, are only accepted with a good reason, so explain why you are late.

What to write

  • Name each activity you disagree with, the points you got, and the descriptor you think applies.
  • Explain why, with real examples: what happens, how often, who helps.
  • Use the "reliably" rule: you must be able to do something safely, to an acceptable standard, repeatedly and in a reasonable time.
  • Point out errors in the assessment report. Ask for a copy if you don't have it.

Is it worth it?

About 1 in 4 mandatory reconsiderations change the award (DWP statistics). If not, you can appeal: in October to December 2025, 64% of PIP appeals heard by a tribunal went the claimant's way (Ministry of Justice).

Free help: Citizens Advice and your council's welfare rights service.
