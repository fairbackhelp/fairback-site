A refused or stalled pet insurance claim is stressful, especially with a vet bill already paid. This page explains the usual steps and who can help for free. It is general information, not advice on your claim.

1. Get the reason in writing

Ask the insurer to tell you in writing why the claim was refused and which policy wording they relied on. Read that wording yourself, alongside your policy schedule. Common reasons include pre-existing conditions, exclusions, the policy type (for example a time-limited or maximum-benefit policy that has reached its limit) and missing paperwork.

2. What insurers are expected to do

The FCA's rules for insurers (ICOBS 8.1) say they must handle claims promptly and fairly, give reasonable guidance and information on progress, not unreasonably reject a claim, and settle promptly once a claim is agreed.

If a refusal is about something said or not said when you bought the policy, the Consumer Insurance (Disclosure and Representations) Act 2012 sets out what an insurer can do. What it can do depends on whether any mistake was honest, careless or deliberate.

3. Make a complaint to the insurer

If you disagree, you can make a formal complaint. Keep copies of the vet's notes, invoices, the claim form and every letter or email, with dates. The insurer generally has up to 8 weeks to send its final response.

4. The Financial Ombudsman Service

If you're unhappy with the final response, or 8 weeks have passed, you can ask the Financial Ombudsman Service to look at it. It's free. You normally need to contact it within 6 months of the insurer's final response. Phone: 0800 023 4567.

Free helplines

  • Citizens Advice consumer helpline (England and Wales): 0808 223 1133 (Welsh language 0808 223 1144)
  • Advice Direct Scotland: 0808 800 9060
  • Consumerline (Northern Ireland): 0300 123 6262
  • Disputes about vet fees or service (not insurance): Veterinary Client Mediation Service, 0345 040 5834, free

Sources: Financial Ombudsman Service "How to complain"; FCA Handbook ICOBS 8.1; Consumer Insurance (Disclosure and Representations) Act 2012; GOV.UK consumer advice; Veterinary Client Mediation Service.
