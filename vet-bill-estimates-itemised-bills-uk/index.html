Vet bills: written estimates and itemised bills under the new rules (UK)

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules change, so check your own letters and GOV.UK, including what you read here. Last checked: November 2026.

After its investigation into the vets market, the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) made an Order that changes what vets must tell you about prices. This page explains what's changing and when. It is general information, not advice.

What's changing

  • Price lists: practices must publish prices for common services
  • Written estimates: for treatment likely to cost £500 or more (including VAT), unless it's urgent or you say you don't want one
  • Itemised bills: showing what you were charged for
  • Prescriptions: written prescription fees are capped at £21.40 for the first item and £12.70 for each extra item on the same prescription
  • Complaints: a written response within 8 weeks, and mediation if you want it

When

Large vet businesses (15 or more practices) start first: price lists from December 2026, the prescription cap and complaints rules from March 2027, and estimates and itemised bills from June 2027. Smaller practices follow, mostly between March and September 2027. Ask your practice which dates apply to them.

What to keep

  • Every estimate and bill, with the date and what it covered
  • Notes of what you were told about options and costs
  • Your pet insurance claim numbers and what each claim covered

If you have a complaint, ask the practice first. The Veterinary Client Mediation Service is free and independent: 0345 040 5834.

Sources: CMA, Veterinary Services Market Investigation Order 2026; GOV.UK guidance for large and small veterinary businesses on complying with the Order (October 2026); vetmediation.co.uk.
