25 money questions to ask before marriage

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Laws and services differ by state and country, so check your own documents and official sources. Last checked: November 2026.

Money is one of the most common things couples disagree about, and one of the easiest to talk about early, before it becomes a problem. These questions are a starting point for an honest conversation. They are not legal or financial advice.

Where you are now

  • What do we each own: savings, pensions or retirement accounts, property, a business?
  • What do we each owe: student loans, credit cards, car finance, anything else?
  • What are our credit histories like, and is there anything the other should know?
  • Do either of us support anyone else financially?

Day to day

  • Joint account, separate accounts, or both?
  • How will we split bills: equally, by income, or another way?
  • What size of purchase should we talk about first?
  • Are we savers or spenders, and how did our families handle money?
  • How much do we each want for personal spending, no questions asked?

Bigger plans

  • Where do we want to live, and do we want to rent or buy?
  • Do we want children, and how would we handle childcare costs and career breaks?
  • How do we feel about one of us stopping work or retraining?
  • What are our goals for the next 5 and 10 years?
  • How would we handle an inheritance or a gift from family?
  • Would we support our parents if they needed it?

If things change

  • What would we want to happen if one of us died? Do our wills reflect that?
  • Who are the beneficiaries on our pensions, life insurance and accounts?
  • Do we want a formal agreement (a prenup)? If so, each of us would speak to our own lawyer.
  • What would a fair approach look like to each of us if we ever separated?

Making it easier

  • Spread it over a few conversations
  • Write down what you agree, and what you still need to talk about
  • Be honest about debts: surprises later cause more harm

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, marriage usually cancels an existing will, unless it was made expecting that marriage. Rules elsewhere differ, so check where you live.
