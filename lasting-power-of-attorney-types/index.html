Lasting Power of Attorney: the two types, cost and how long it takes

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules change, so check your own letters and GOV.UK, including what you read here. Last checked: November 2026.

A Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) is a legal document that lets you choose one or more people (your attorneys) to make decisions for you if you can't make them yourself. You must be 18 or over and have mental capacity when you make it. These rules apply in England and Wales; Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own systems.

The two types

  • Health and welfare: decisions about things like medical care, moving into a care home and daily routine.
  • Property and financial affairs: decisions about money and property, such as paying bills or managing a bank account.

You can make one type or both.

Cost and timing

Registration costs £92 for each LPA, unless you qualify for a reduction or exemption. GOV.UK says registration takes 8 to 10 weeks if there are no mistakes in the application, so it helps to do it before it's needed.

Tell people where it is

Once registered, keep it safe and tell your attorneys and close family where to find it.
