Care home fees and the family home: the 12-week property disregard explained (England)

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules change, so check your own letters and GOV.UK, including what you read here. Last checked: November 2026.

When someone moves permanently into a care home in England and the council may help pay, it does a financial assessment. Whether the person's home is counted depends on who still lives there and how long the person has been in care.

The first 12 weeks

The value of the person's main or only home is ignored for the first 12 weeks of a permanent care home placement.

When the home is not counted at all

  • A partner or former partner still lives there (unless estranged).
  • A relative aged 60 or over, or who is incapacitated, lives there.
  • A child under 18 whom the person is liable to support lives there.
  • The council can also choose to ignore it in other cases.

The 2026/27 capital limits

Over £23,250 in capital, the person usually pays the full fees. Between £14,250 and £23,250, the council helps and £1 a week is counted for every £250 above £14,250.

Deferred payments

If the home is counted, a deferred payment agreement may let the council pay now and recover the money later, usually when the home is sold. Councils must offer one to people who meet the criteria. Interest can be charged.

Sources: Age UK factsheets 10 and 38; NHS, "When the council might pay for your care". Free help: Age UK Advice Line 0800 678 1602.
