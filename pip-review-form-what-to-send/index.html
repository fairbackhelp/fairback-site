PIP review form: what to send back, and by when

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules change, so check your own letters and GOV.UK, including what you read here. Last checked: November 2026.

If you get PIP, DWP will review your award from time to time. You'll get a letter asking you to fill in a form called "Personal Independence Payment Review". This page explains the steps. It is general information, not advice.

The deadline

GOV.UK says to return the form within 1 month; some advice sites say 4 weeks. Go by the date on your own letter. If you need more time, contact the PIP enquiry line as soon as you can and explain why. If the form isn't returned on time without a good reason, DWP may stop your PIP.

What to send

  • The completed form, describing how your condition affects you now, on a typical day and on bad days
  • Any supporting information you haven't already shared with DWP: new letters, reports, prescriptions, care plans
  • The form explains what to include and where to send it

Habits that help

  • Keep a copy of everything you send, and note the date you posted it (proof of postage is useful)
  • List your evidence in one place: what it is, who it's from and the date
  • Write down every call: date, who you spoke to and what was said

What happens next

  • You keep getting PIP while your claim is being reviewed
  • A health professional may phone you, or invite you to an assessment in person, by phone or by video
  • Your award might stay the same, go up, go down or stop
  • If you disagree with the decision, you can ask for a mandatory reconsideration

Free help: Citizens Advice, your council's welfare rights service, or the Turn2us adviser finder.

Sources: GOV.UK "PIP: when your claim is reviewed"; Turn2us "Review or renew a PIP claim" (reviewed August 2026).
