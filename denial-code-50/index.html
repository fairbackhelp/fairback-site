Denied as "not medically necessary" (code 50): how to appeal

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules differ by plan and state, so check your own documents and official sources, including what you read here. Law last checked: 20 November 2026.

Short answer: code 50 means the insurer decided the care doesn't meet its medical necessity rules. The way forward is to show, point by point, that it does.

What to do

  1. Ask for the claim file and the exact criteria used to deny you. You're entitled to them free of charge.
  2. Ask your doctor to request a peer-to-peer review, where they talk directly with the insurer's reviewer.
  3. Ask your doctor for a letter of medical necessity that answers each criterion: diagnosis, treatments already tried and why they failed, the risk of waiting, and supporting guidelines.
  4. File the internal appeal with the letter and records attached.
  5. Still denied? Request an external review by an independent reviewer, within 4 months of the final internal denial. If the reviewer sides with you, the plan must pay.
