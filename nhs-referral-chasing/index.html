Waiting to hear about an NHS referral? Who to ask and what to note

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules change, so check your own letters and GOV.UK, including what you read here. Last checked: November 2026.

Many people find the hardest part of an NHS referral is the silence afterwards. Knowing who holds the referral at each stage makes it easier to ask the right person.

Who to ask

  • Was it sent? Your GP surgery can tell you whether the referral was sent, when and to which service.
  • Once it has arrived: the hospital department or its booking team holds your place on the list. The number is usually on any letter you've had.
  • e-Referral Service: if you were referred this way, you can book, view and change your first appointment in the NHS App (age 16 and over).
  • A concern about how it's going: PALS (Patient Advice and Liaison Service) at the hospital can help, and can explain the complaints process.

The 18-week standard (England)

The NHS Constitution for England gives you the right to start consultant-led treatment within a maximum of 18 weeks from referral for non-urgent conditions, or for the NHS to take all reasonable steps to offer a range of suitable alternative providers if that isn't possible. Some exceptions apply. Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own waiting time rules.

What to write down each time

  • The date you were referred and where to.
  • Any reference number on your letters.
  • Each time you ask: the date, who you spoke to, and what they said would happen next.

Sources: NHS England, "Referral to treatment"; nhs.uk, "Using the e-Referral Service in the NHS App" and "What is PALS". In an emergency call 999; for urgent help use NHS 111.
