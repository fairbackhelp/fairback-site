Carer's assessment: what it is and what to have ready (England)

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules change, so check your own letters and GOV.UK, including what you read here. Last checked: November 2026.

If you look after someone who is ill, older or disabled, you can ask your local council for a carer's assessment. The NHS says it's free and anyone over 18 can ask for one. It looks at how caring affects you, and it is separate from the cared-for person's own needs assessment. You can ask for both to be done at the same time.

How to ask

Contact adult social services at your local council by phone or online. (For a disabled child, contact the council's children with disabilities team.) GOV.UK has a tool to find your council.

What to have ready

  • Your NHS number (if you have it) and your GP's details
  • The cared-for person's name, address, date of birth and NHS number (if available)
  • Contact details for anyone who will join you
  • Your email address
  • Notes on how caring affects your health, work, sleep, free time and relationships, with real examples

It's usually face to face, sometimes by phone or online, and often lasts at least an hour.

What can follow

Examples the NHS lists include respite cover, help with housework or gardening, taxi fares, training in safe lifting, local support groups and advice on carer benefits. If you qualify for help, the council writes a care and support plan with you. Some help may depend on a financial assessment. If you don't qualify, the council should still give you free advice about local help.

Free helplines

  • Carers UK: 0808 808 7777
  • Age UK: 0800 678 1602
  • Independent Age: 0800 319 6789

If you're unhappy with how the council handled it, complain to the council first; after that you can go to the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman.

Source: NHS "Carer's assessments" (nhs.uk). This covers England; Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own arrangements.
