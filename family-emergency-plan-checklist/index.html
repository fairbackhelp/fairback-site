A family emergency plan means everyone knows what to do, who to call and where to find important information. It takes an evening to put together.

The checklist

  • Contacts: family, neighbours, doctors, school and work, plus one contact who lives further away
  • Meeting points: one near home and one outside your area
  • Medical: conditions, medicines and doses, allergies, and insurance or health numbers
  • Children and dependants: who can collect them, and school or care contacts
  • Pets: vet details, microchip number and who could look after them
  • Home: where the water shut-off, gas valve and fuse box are
  • Documents: where originals are kept: ID, insurance, wills, powers of attorney
  • Money: which bank holds what (institution and last 4 digits only, never full numbers or passwords)

Make it work

  • Talk it through with everyone in the household
  • Keep a copy somewhere safe, and tell two trusted people where it is
  • Check it once a year

For local hazards and official advice, check your national or local emergency service's website.
