Caring for a parent: the information to gather now

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Laws and services differ by state and country, so check your own documents and official sources. Last checked: November 2026.

When a parent needs more help, the hardest part is often finding information in a hurry. Gathering it calmly, with your parent's agreement, saves stress later. This checklist is about information only.

People to know

  • Doctors, specialists, pharmacy and dentist, with phone numbers
  • Neighbors, friends and anyone with a spare key
  • Who else in the family helps, and with what

Health

  • Current medicines, doses and who prescribed them
  • Allergies and main conditions
  • Upcoming appointments and tests
  • Insurance or health cards (note where they are kept, not the numbers)

Home and money

  • Regular bills and how they're paid
  • Which banks and providers they use (names only; never write down passwords)
  • Utilities, home insurance and who to call for repairs

Documents: where they are

  • ID, birth and marriage certificates
  • Will, and any power of attorney or advance directive
  • Property and pension papers

Keep it in one place

One binder that you, your parent and one trusted person know about beats papers spread across drawers. Review it every few months.
