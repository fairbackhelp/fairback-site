Vet prescriptions and fees in the UK: what is changing

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Rules change, so check your own letters and GOV.UK, including what you read here. Last checked: November 2026.

In 2026 the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) finished its market investigation into UK vet services. It found average vet prices rose about 63% from 2016 to 2023, compared with about 32% general inflation, and that online pharmacies typically charge 50 to 60% less for medicines.

Written prescriptions

You can ask your vet for a written prescription and buy the medicine from another registered pharmacy or retailer. Under the new rules, practices must tell you this, and prescription fees will be capped (the CMA set £21 for the first medicine and £12.50 for each extra one, rising with inflation).

Other changes being phased in

  • Published price lists for common treatments (large groups from December 2026, smaller practices into 2027).
  • A written estimate for treatment likely to cost £500 or more.
  • Itemised bills.
  • Clear signs showing whether a practice belongs to a large group.

If something goes wrong

Raise it with the practice first. The Veterinary Client Mediation Service offers free mediation (0345 040 5834).

Sources: CMA final report summary (March 2026); RCVS. Always follow your vet's clinical advice.
