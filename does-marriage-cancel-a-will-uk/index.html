Many people are surprised to learn that getting married can cancel the will they already have.

The general rule

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, getting married or forming a civil partnership usually revokes an existing will. The exception is a will made in expectation of that particular marriage or civil partnership.

If the will is revoked and no new one is made, the estate is shared under the intestacy rules, which may not match what the person wanted.

Unmarried partners

In England and Wales, a partner who isn't married or in a civil partnership often gets nothing automatically if there is no will.

What couples often do

  • Talk about what each of you would want to happen
  • Note where your current wills are kept and when they were made
  • Ask a solicitor whether to make new wills before or after the wedding

Scotland has different rules; check with a Scottish solicitor.

Sources: GOV.UK "Marriages and civil partnerships: legal rights and responsibilities"; GOV.UK "Who inherits if someone dies without a will"; HMRC Inheritance Tax Manual IHTM12074. This page is general information, not legal advice.
