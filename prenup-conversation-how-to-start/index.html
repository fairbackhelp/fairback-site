How to bring up a prenup with your partner (and what to talk about first)

Self-help information, for educational purposes only. Not legal, medical or financial advice. Laws and services differ by state and country, so check your own documents and official sources. Last checked: November 2026.

Talking about money before marriage is less about the paperwork and more about understanding each other. Whether or not you end up with a prenuptial agreement, the conversation itself helps. This page is about having that conversation. It is not legal advice.

When and how to raise it

  • Pick a calm, unhurried moment, well before the wedding, not in the middle of planning stress
  • Frame it as looking after each other and planning together
  • Say why it matters to you, in your own words
  • Agree that you'll each be open about what you have and what you owe
  • Expect more than one conversation

Topics couples often talk through first

  • What each of you owns and owes now
  • How you'll handle day-to-day money: joint, separate or both
  • Homes, businesses, savings and anything inherited or expected
  • Children, including any from earlier relationships
  • What you'd want to happen if one of you died, and whether your wills need updating
  • Career breaks, caring and how you'd share the impact

The rules depend on where you live

Whether and how an agreement is recognised depends on your state, province or country. In many places both partners need to sign, give full and honest disclosure, and may need independent legal advice. If you decide to make a formal agreement, each of you should speak to your own lawyer.

Marriage and wills

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, getting married or forming a civil partnership usually cancels an existing will, unless it was made expecting that marriage. Rules elsewhere differ, so check where you live.
