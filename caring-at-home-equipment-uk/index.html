When a parent starts needing care at home, there are hundreds of products that might help. These are the things carers mention again and again, in the order most families need them. Get the free options first.

Free first

  • Care needs assessment from your council's adult social care team (England). An occupational therapist may visit and recommend equipment. Aids and minor adaptations up to £1,000 each are free if your parent is assessed as needing them.
  • Hospital beds, hoists and commodes are often lent by the local community equipment service. Ask the OT or district nurse before you buy.
  • VAT relief: if your parent is disabled or has a long-term illness, some equipment bought for their own use can be sold without VAT. Ask the seller.
  • Continence products may be available through the NHS continence service after an assessment.

Things carers say they wish they'd had sooner

  • Bed and bedroom: waterproof bed pads, a slide sheet or draw sheet for moving someone in bed, an overbed table for meals and supplies.
  • Moving safely: grab rails, a raised toilet seat or frame, and a second walking frame kept where they're needed. Ask an OT before using a transfer belt or standing aid.
  • Night-time: motion-sensor night lights for the route to the bathroom, and a bed or door sensor if they wander.
  • Days and memory: a large day-and-date clock and a whiteboard for the day's plan.

What to watch out for

  • Very cheap walking aids and shower stools: carers report them cracking. Look for a stated user weight limit.
  • Anything that clamps or bolts on: check it fits your bath, bed or toilet first.
  • Bed rails: only use with advice, as poorly fitted rails can cause injury.

Where to look

Washable bed pads, overbed tables, slide sheets, grab rails, raised toilet seats, motion-sensor night lights and day-and-date clocks.
